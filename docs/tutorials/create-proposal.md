Learn how to create and submit a governance proposal on BelizeChain - from small community projects to major blockchain upgrades.

**What you'll learn**:
- Types of proposals

- Submit to blockchain
- Promote to voters

## 🎯 Types of Proposals

### 1. Treasury Proposals 💰
**Request funding from BelizeChain Treasury**

**Budget**: 3.2M bBZD available  
**Pass Rate**: 62%  
**Typical Amount**: 10K - 500K bBZD

**Examples**:
- Community center construction
- Tourism marketing campaign
- Healthcare AI system
- Education programs
- Infrastructure projects

**Requirements**:
- Detailed budget breakdown
- Clear outcomes and milestones
- Timeline with deliverables
- Team qualifications

---

### 2. Technical Proposals ⚙️
**Upgrade blockchain parameters or features**

**Pass Rate**: 81%  
**Typical Changes**:
- Block size/time adjustments
- Fee structure changes
- New pallet features
- Runtime upgrades

**Examples**:
- Reduce block time 6s → 4s
- Increase block size 3MB → 5MB
- Add new staking features
- Improve quantum integration

**Requirements**:
- Technical specifications
- Performance impact analysis
- Security audit (if needed)
- Developer consensus

---

### 3. Policy Proposals 📜
**Change rules and regulations**

**Pass Rate**: 58%  
**Typical Changes**:
- Fee adjustments
- Staking requirements
- Voting thresholds
- Compliance rules

**Examples**:
- Reduce transaction fees 50%
- Change validator minimum to 5K DALLA
- Adjust voting period to 14 days
- Update KYC requirements

**Requirements**:
- Legal compliance review
- Impact analysis
- Community consultation
- FSC approval (if financial)

---

### 4. District Proposals 🏘️
**Local decisions for specific districts**

**Pass Rate**: 72%  
**Budget**: Typically 5K - 50K bBZD  
**Districts**: San Pedro, Belize City, Belmopan, Dangriga, Orange Walk, Punta Gorda

**Examples**:
- Beach cleanup San Pedro
- Public WiFi Belize City
- Youth center Dangriga
- Market upgrade Punta Gorda

**Requirements**:
- Local support (signatures)
- District council approval
- Realistic budget
- Implementation plan

---

### 5. Emergency Proposals 🚨
**Urgent fixes (security, critical bugs)**

**Voting Period**: 24-48 hours (vs 7 days)  
**Threshold**: 67% approval (vs 50%)  
**Pass Rate**: 94%

**Examples**:
- Security patch
- Critical bug fix
- Emergency funding
- Network recovery

**Requirements**:
- Validator consensus
- Clear urgency justification
- Technical analysis
- Immediate action plan

## 📝 Writing Your Proposal

### The 7-Section Format

All proposals must include these sections:

#### 1. Title
**Clear, specific, under 80 characters**

✅ **Good Examples**:
- "Fund 80K bBZD Community Center in Dangriga"
- "Reduce Block Time from 6 to 4 Seconds"
- "Tourism Marketing Campaign - 250K Budget"

❌ **Bad Examples**:
- "Community Improvement" (too vague)
- "Make BelizeChain Better" (no specifics)
- "Money for Project" (unprofessional)

---

#### 2. Summary
**One paragraph, 2-3 sentences**

What, why, how much, when.

**Example (Treasury Proposal)**:
> "This proposal requests 80,000 bBZD to build a youth community center in Dangriga, serving 500+ youth with education programs, sports facilities, and job training. The center will be completed in 6 months and operated by the Dangriga Youth Foundation. Total cost includes construction (60K), equipment (15K), and first-year operations (5K)."

---

#### 3. Description
**Detailed explanation, 3-5 paragraphs**

**Include**:
- **Problem**: What issue does this solve?
- **Solution**: How does your proposal fix it?
- **Benefits**: Who benefits and how?
- **Details**: Specific information
- **Evidence**: Data, research, examples

**Example Structure**:
```
Paragraph 1: The Problem
"Dangriga lacks safe recreational spaces for youth ages 12-18. 
Current facilities are overcrowded, unsafe, and limited to 
school hours only. This contributes to youth unemployment (35%) 
and limited educational opportunities."

Paragraph 2: Our Solution
"The proposed community center will provide 3,000 sq ft of 
dedicated space including computer lab, sports area, meeting 
rooms, and outdoor basketball court. Open 6 days/week, 2-9 PM."

Paragraph 3: Who Benefits
"500+ youth will have access to free after-school programs, 
job training, tutoring, and sports. 20 part-time jobs created 
for coaches and instructors."

Paragraph 4: Implementation Plan
"Construction starts May 2026, completion November 2026. 
Dangriga Youth Foundation (DYF) will manage operations. DYF 
has 10 years experience running youth programs."

Paragraph 5: Long-term Impact
"Similar centers in other districts show 40% improvement in 
youth employment within 2 years. Academic performance improves 
25% for participants."
```

---

**Itemized costs (for Treasury proposals)**

**Format**:
```

╠════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  Construction:                             ║
║  - Building construction      45,000 bBZD  ║
║  - Electrical/plumbing        8,000 bBZD   ║
║  - Painting/finishing         4,000 bBZD   ║
║  - Basketball court           3,000 bBZD   ║
║  Subtotal Construction:      60,000 bBZD   ║
║                                            ║
║  Equipment:                                ║
║  - 15 computers + software    8,000 bBZD   ║
║  - Sports equipment           3,000 bBZD   ║
║  - Furniture (tables, chairs) 2,500 bBZD   ║
║  - Office equipment           1,500 bBZD   ║
║  Subtotal Equipment:         15,000 bBZD   ║
║                                            ║
║  First-Year Operations:                    ║
║  - Staff salaries (6 months)  3,000 bBZD   ║
║  - Utilities (6 months)       1,000 bBZD   ║
║  - Program materials            500 bBZD   ║
║  - Insurance                    500 bBZD   ║
║  Subtotal Operations:         5,000 bBZD   ║
║                                            ║
║  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━  ║
║  TOTAL REQUESTED:            80,000 bBZD   ║
╚════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

**Tips**:
- Be specific (not "supplies: 10K")
- Include contingency (5-10%)
- Get quotes from contractors
- Justify major expenses
- Show how funds are released (milestones)

---

#### 5. Timeline
**When things happen**

**Format**:
```
Phase 1: Planning (Month 1-2)
- Secure property lease
- Finalize architectural plans
- Hire contractor
- Order equipment

Phase 2: Construction (Month 3-6)
- Building construction
- Install electrical/plumbing
- Build basketball court
- Setup computer lab

Phase 3: Setup (Month 7)
- Install equipment
- Hire staff
- Train instructors
- Safety inspection

Phase 4: Launch (Month 8)
- Grand opening event
- Begin programs
- Enrollment
- Community outreach

Ongoing: Operations
- Daily programs
- Quarterly reports to community
- Annual financial audit
```

**Include**:
- Realistic timeframes
- Dependencies
- Milestones for fund release
- Reporting schedule

---

#### 6. Expected Outcomes
**Measurable results**

**Format**:
```
Year 1 (2026-2027):
✓ 500+ youth enrolled
✓ 20 part-time jobs created
✓ 80% attendance rate
✓ 10 weekly programs running

Year 2 (2027-2028):
✓ 750+ youth enrolled (+50%)
✓ 90% participant satisfaction
✓ 25% improvement in grades (students)
✓ 30% reduction in youth unemployment (participants)

Year 3 (2028-2029):
✓ Self-sustaining operations (grants + fees)
✓ 1,000+ total youth served
✓ Model replicated in other districts
✓ National recognition
```

**Make them**:
- Specific and measurable
- Realistic and achievable
- Time-bound
- Verifiable

---

#### 7. Risks & Mitigation
**What could go wrong and how you'll handle it**

**Example**:
```
Risk 1: Construction Delays
Likelihood: Medium
Impact: Low
Mitigation: 
- 10% time buffer in schedule
- Penalty clauses in contractor agreement
- Regular progress monitoring

Risk 2: Lower Enrollment Than Expected
Likelihood: Low
Impact: Medium
Mitigation:
- Extensive marketing campaign
- Partnerships with schools
- Free trial programs
- Transportation provided

Risk 3: Funding Insufficient
Likelihood: Low
Impact: High
Mitigation:
- 5% contingency in budget
- Secondary funding sources identified
- Phased rollout if needed
- Community fundraising backup

Risk 4: Staff Turnover
Likelihood: Medium
Impact: Medium
Mitigation:
- Competitive salaries
- Training programs
- Clear advancement path
- Backup staff identified
```

- Review feedback
- Revise proposal

[View Feedback] [Analyze Results] [Plan Revision]

**What to do**:

1. **Don't give up!** Many successful proposals failed first attempt
2. **Read feedback** carefully (comments, votes)
3. **Identify issues**:
   - Budget too high?
   - Timeline unrealistic?
   - Unclear benefits?
   - Wrong timing?
4. **Revise proposal** based on feedback
5. **Get more community input** before resubmitting
6. **Resubmit** with improvements (30-day wait)

**Success stories**:
- Carlos's Marketing: Failed 28% → Revised → Passed 71%
- San Pedro Beach Cleanup: Failed 45% → Revised → Passed 82%

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

### Problem: Can't Submit Proposal

**Causes**:
- Insufficient DALLA (need 100+ deposit)
- KYC not completed
- Network issues
- Wallet not synced

**Solutions**:
1. Check balance (need 100 DALLA + fees)
2. Complete KYC Level 1+ (Settings → Verification)
3. Check internet connection
4. Sync wallet (may take 5 minutes)
5. Try again or contact support

---

### Problem: Low Vote Turnout

**Causes**:
- Poor promotion
- Unclear proposal
- Wrong timing
- Community not engaged

**Solutions**:
1. Increase promotion (daily posts)
2. Simplify explanation
3. Answer ALL questions
4. Host Q&A events
5. Reach out to validators
6. Use visual aids (videos, infographics)
7. Share success stories

---

### Problem: Negative Feedback

**Causes**:
- Budget concerns
- Unclear benefits
- Competing proposals
- Timing issues

**Solutions**:
1. Address concerns directly
2. Provide more details
3. Adjust budget if possible
4. Emphasize benefits
5. Build coalition with other proposals
6. Be open to criticism
7. Stay professional

---

### Problem: Technical Questions You Can't Answer

**Solutions**:
1. Be honest: "I don't know, let me find out"
2. Consult experts
3. Post in #technical channel (Discord)
4. Tag validators with expertise
5. Update proposal with answer
6. Don't guess or make up answers
